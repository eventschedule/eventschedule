name isn't.

- run the code, change it, and sell a service built on it, under the [LICENSE](LICENSE);
- keep the attribution the licence requires (the name "Hillel Coren", "Event Schedule" and
  https://www.eventschedule.com), which the app shows as a small credit on public schedule pages;
- say your service is "Powered by Event Schedule", "runs on Event Schedule" or "based on Event
  Schedule", as the credit (not as your brand);
- run an install for your own organisation and call it Event Schedule inside it;
- write about the project.

**You may not:**

- name a public service, website, domain, app or app store listing "Event Schedule" or anything
  confusingly close;
- use the Event Schedule logo as your logo or app icon;
- suggest that we endorse, run or support your service;
- use eventschedule.com's pages, words, screenshots or logo in your own marketing, so nobody
  mistakes your service for ours, or ours for yours.

A platform built on the code that is offered to the public (a SaaS, a reseller, an agency's own
product) changes the name, the logo, the app icon and the apps' bundle identifiers. The logo's
files are `public/images/logo.*`, `public/images/dark_logo.*` and `public/images/light_logo.*`;
the [SaaS setup guide](https://eventschedule.com/docs/saas#branding) says where to put your own.

This makes concrete the line the licence's clause 3 draws: "Neither the name nor any trademark of
the Author may be used to endorse or promote products derived from this software without specific
prior written permission."
